# Lecture11: Ethics & Professional Conduct

Ethics and professional conduct in research are about ensuring that how science is done is as important as what is discovered. Good research requires moral responsibility, honest scientific practice, and professional behavior.

Modern research systems increasingly emphasize openness, transparency, and responsibility, with policies pushing for open science, open data, and ethical training.

Professional conduct frameworks (like CNRS rules) focus on respecting people, acting honestly, avoiding bias, protecting confidential information, and maintaining independence while respecting organizational structure.

Conflicts of interest can distort science through funding influence, personal relationships, or hidden agendas, so transparency and disclosure are essential. Some actors may even deliberately create doubt about established science.

Ethical thinking should happen before research starts, not just after problems appear.

Historical cases show that technically rigorous research can still be unethical if participants are harmed or deceived. This led to modern frameworks requiring informed consent, risk minimization, and fair treatment.

Technology and research are never neutral; design decisions can reinforce inequality or shift power and resources.

Scientific misconduct includes fabricating data, altering results, plagiarism, or falsely claiming credit, and usually leads to severe professional and legal consequences.

Modern research must also protect personal data and consider environmental impact and long term societal effects.

Main takeaway:
Ethics is not a final checklist step. It must be built into research design, methods, and decisions from the beginning.
